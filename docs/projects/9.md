# Project brief — Transport groups and tape labels

The mandate is [#9](https://github.com/yedeya/playground-3d/issues/9)'s body (`K-PROJ-15`). It builds on two sibling projects: [#6](https://github.com/yedeya/playground-3d/issues/6) moves the app to TypeScript, Next.js and SST (its brief merged from PR #11), and [#8](https://github.com/yedeya/playground-3d/issues/8) adds the backend, the owner's auth and the offline PWA (its brief merged from PR #15). The acceptance criteria are the `TRANS` clauses in `docs/qa/specs/transport.md`, seeded in this pull request. Eight decisions in §7 belonged to a person (1 to 6, 9 and 11), two of them the questions the mandate names, and the Owner and the Maintainer have answered all eight; three are technical (7, 8 and 10), and I settled them.

## 1. The real problem

**The mandate reads as three features (groups, a filter and overview, tape labels), but it is one problem: the app has no data of its own.** Everything it shows today is the generated model, bundled into the client, and nothing the owner decides can be stored anywhere:

- **The app stores nothing.** `git grep -nE "fetch\(|localStorage|indexedDB" -- src` prints nothing. The store holds three UI fields, `filter`, `selected` and `natural`, and loses them on reload (`git grep -n "createStore(" -- src`).
- **There is no notion of a piece, a joint or a label.** `git grep -niE "transport|joint" -- src` prints nothing. The word `tape` appears only as the CSS class of the module swatch (`git grep -n "tape" -- src`), so the module colours are drawn *as* tape strips, but nothing says they are the tape on the real playset (decision 4).
- **The only identity a stored record can point at is the part `id`, and that is generated.** The ids are this project's own codes, not markings on the wood (`docs/HANDOVER.md`), and #7 is about to regenerate the whole model in another language. #7's mandate requires the ids to survive, and `[APP-1]` (#6's merged brief) pins the count, not the names. So a plan that refers to parts by id must survive an id that disappears without losing anything silently (`[TRANS-2]`, `[TRANS-7]`).
- **The geometry under the labels had to be settled first.** The ladder and slide are mirrored against the manual by a flag in the generator (`git grep -n "SWAP_LADDER_SLIDE =" -- model`), and `docs/HANDOVER.md` recorded that nobody knew whether that is how the real playset stands or how the owner wants it at the new place. A label is a point on a part, so labels recorded on the wrong geometry would be wrong exactly where the rebuild needs them. Decision 2 settles it: the real playset stands swapped, so the model is already the geometry the labels go on, and nothing here changes it (`[TRANS-19]`).

**So the systemic fix is a small transport-plan domain first, and everything else on top of it.** Issue A writes the plan's rules as pure functions: membership, unknown parts, the delete rule, contacts between pieces, the label's shape, the palette and displaced labels (`[TRANS-1]` to `[TRANS-7]`). Storage (Issue C), the view (D), editing (E), the overview (F) and labels (G) each read those rules rather than re-deriving them. The contacts in `[TRANS-4]` are what turn "record the tape on the joints" into something the app can check: the plan overview counts the neighbour pairs that still have no label (`[TRANS-12]`), and that count reaching zero on the real playset is the project's delivery (`[TRANS-20]`).

**The pieces this model suggests, measured rather than guessed.** Parts per module: `grep -o '"module":"[A-Z0-9]*"' src/data/speeltoestel.json | sort | uniq -c` prints 63 in `A45`, 34 in `A47`, 16 in `B07`, 10 in `F34`, 8 each in `C14` and `G28`, 6 each in `D42` and `E24`, 5 in `G24`, 4 in `H10` and 1 in `G01`, which is 161. The generator's comments place the climbing holds (`G24`) on the ship's wall, the shields (`H10`) on the bridge rails, and the bridge belts clamped over a house beam at one end and a ship beam at the other (`git grep -nE "ship tower|belts clamp|H10 shields" -- model`). So a module is not always a piece: some modules ride on another module's piece, and one (the bridge) has a joint at both ends. Decision 1 records that the app proposes no pieces: the plan starts empty and the Owner defines the pieces at the playset, so this measurement is background for Issue H, not data the app ships.

**Reconciliation gap: none in this area, and the one that exists is #6's Issue A.** On `main` there is no spec at all, and `kanon-spec-coverage` reports `TRANS` with 20 clauses, 0 locked and 0 `[confirmed]` without a test. It also reports that `docs/qa/stack.md` declares no `## Code areas`, so no test counts as evidence yet. #6's brief makes declaring them its Issue A, and this project waits for #6 (decision 3), so it would be a duplicate here.

## 2. Scope

**This project is:**

- the transport plan: named pieces with a colour, each part in at most one piece, and joint labels with a tape colour, a short text and a point on a part;
- the plan stored in #8's backend against the owner's account, readable from every device and editable offline through #8's sync;
- the plan in the 3D view: a chip per piece in the filter row, a transport colour mode, and label markers;
- an overview per piece (parts, size, neighbours, labels) and one for the whole plan (what is still loose, unknown, unlabelled or displaced);
- editing the plan on a phone: pieces, tap-to-assign, whole modules, and placing labels;
- the owner's own pass over the real playset: entering the plan, taping, labelling, and a phone check.

**It is not:**

- **The backend itself, auth or offline support.** Those are #8's. This project adds its own tables and reads and writes through #8's API, auth and sync, and changes none of them (`[TRANS-16]` to `[TRANS-18]`). If #8 ships without a way for a feature to add tables, that is a gap in #8, filed there.
- **The stack move, the generator port, or any change to the model.** #6 and #7. Decision 2 records that the model already shows the ladder and slide as the real playset stands, so nothing here touches the model.
- **The new place.** Showing the playset at its new place and comparing the safety zones (which `docs/HANDOVER.md` says nobody has done) are a structural check by a person on site, not an app feature.
- **Weights, a packing list or a van layout.** The overview gives each piece's size (`[TRANS-11]`); weight is not in the model, and estimating it is guesswork.
- **Photos, a printable or a PDF export of the plan.** Months-later recall rests on the backend and the offline app. A paper copy is a reasonable follow-up if the owner wants one in the van; it is out only because nobody asked for it.
- **Checking the model's backs against photos.** `docs/HANDOVER.md` lists it as open; it changes geometry, not the plan, and the plan survives a geometry change by `[TRANS-7]`.

**Specific to this project's definition of done:**

- **Every clause stays `[seed]`.** Promotion is a later human act (`K-SPEC-8`).
- **`[TRANS-20]` is a person's verdict,** recorded on Issue H. No test can lock it, so expect `verify-acs` to report it as not run. I have not checked how it treats that (§8).
- **The answers are written into the clauses.** `[TRANS-3]` carries decision 9, `[TRANS-6]` decisions 4 and 11, `[TRANS-13]` decision 1, and `[TRANS-19]` decision 2, and every new interface text is English (decision 11). No clause now waits on a decision.
- **Routing.** All of it is the app's own work, so every item is `enhancement` on Product Backlog (`K-WORK-4`). Nothing here is pipeline work.

## 4. Cost

**No delta.** This project adds no infrastructure: its three tables live in #8's database, and its reads and writes go through #8's API. The data is tiny: at most 161 memberships (one per part, by the count in §1), about ten pieces and a few dozen labels, so storage is kilobytes and well inside what #8 already pays for. Requests come only from the owner opening and editing the app, a handful of sessions around one move, so they add nothing measurable to #8's Lambda or database cost.

There is one interaction to name: if #8's Aurora scales to zero, every app open that reads the plan wakes it. That is the same wake #8's own data causes, at the same moments, so it adds no wakes of its own, and nothing here runs on a schedule (`K-OBS-10`). #8's approved range of about $1 to $3 a month is unchanged. This is inferred from #8's mandate; #8 has no brief yet to check it against.

## 5. Observability

**I propose adding no alarm and paging on nothing. That is the Owner's call, so it is decision 6.** The signals this project creates:

- **A plan change that fails to save.** The owner sees it in the app, because `[TRANS-17]` requires an unsaved change to show as not saved. The person who would be paged is the person holding the phone, so an alarm adds nothing. Not paged; the trigger that changes the answer is a second user of the plan.
- **Stored data going stale against the model** (unknown parts, displaced labels). Shown in the plan overview (`[TRANS-12]`), not paged: it can only happen when someone regenerates the model, which is a reviewed pull request.
- **Backend errors and data loss.** #8's to watch. A backup of the plan is what makes the months-later rebuild safe, so I flag it to #8 rather than adding it here.

If #6 has created `docs/observability.md` by the time Issue C runs, Issue C adds the first two as rows of its not-paged table.
